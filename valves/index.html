    Valve Grading Reference

    Parts of this page are wrong: do not use them for patient management (notice, 27 November 2026)

    A check against the ACC/AHA 2020 valve guideline, the ASE/EACVI and ASE/SCMR grading documents and the ESC/EACTS 2025 guideline found errors that could change management. Severity stages and intervention thresholds follow ACC/AHA 2020; echo grading follows ASE/EACVI.

    • Aortic stenosis: a mean gradient under 20 mmHg is mild and 20–39 mmHg moderate (not under 25 and 25–39). Very severe is a Vmax ≥5.0 m/s or a mean gradient ≥60 mmHg; none of ACC/AHA 2020, ASE/EACVI 2017 or ESC/EACTS 2025 uses an AVA under 0.6 cm². In discordant low-gradient AS, use the aortic valve calcium score on non-contrast CT (ACC/AHA 2020: severe at ≥1300 AU in women, ≥2000 AU in men); CT planimetry overestimates the valve area.
    • Aortic regurgitation: a vena contracta of exactly 6 mm is moderate; severe is over 6 mm. By CMR, severe AR is an RF ≥50% (ASE 2017, ACC/AHA 2020); ESC/EACTS 2025 counts a CMR RF over 40% as a criterion of severe AR. The lower thresholds in the outcome table are research findings, not grades.
    • Aortic root and ascending aorta: consider surgery at ≥55 mm in everyone, not men only; at 50–55 mm with a bicuspid valve and risk factors (ACC/AHA); at ≥45 mm only when aortic valve surgery is already indicated. The Marfan figure was not checked.
    • Mitral stenosis: follow the ACC/AHA stages. Severe MS is an MVA ≤1.5 cm² (pressure half-time ≥150 ms); there is no moderate stage, so an MVA of 1.0–1.5 cm² is severe. Mean gradient and PASP are consequences, not grades. The Wilkins score is for balloon commissurotomy, not MitraClip.
    • Secondary MR: ACC/AHA defines severe secondary MR as for primary (EROA ≥0.40 cm², RVol ≥60 mL, RF ≥50%), and transcatheter repair needs severe MR plus the COAPT selection criteria (LVEF 20–50%, LVESD ≤70 mm, PASP ≤70 mmHg, symptoms on guideline-directed therapy). In ESC 2025 an EROA ≥30 mm² or RVol ≥45 mL carries prognostic weight, and severe is an EROA ≥40 mm² (≥30 mm² with an elliptical orifice) and RVol ≥60 mL (≥45 mL with low flow). The page's 0.20 cm², 0.30 cm² and 30 mL thresholds are wrong.
    • MR by CMR: regurgitant volume is LV stroke volume minus aortic total forward flow, not net forward flow, which counts any aortic regurgitant volume as MR.
    • Tricuspid regurgitation: severe by 3D vena contracta area is over 0.4 cm² (ASE 2017; ESC 2025 uses ≥0.75 cm²). The mild and moderate values are in none of the sources checked, and the cited "Adamo et al EHJ 2025" study could not be found.
    • Pulmonary regurgitation by CMR: the RVol bands are in none of the sources checked (ASE/SCMR 2017 gives no RVol grades for PR).

    Sources: ACC/AHA 2020, ASE/EACVI 2017 on aortic stenosis, ASE/SCMR 2017 on regurgitation, EAE/ASE 2009 on stenosis, ESC/EACTS 2025 and CAR/CSTR 2024 Part 2.

    CMR — Outcome-Based Thresholds

    Formal CMR-specific severity grades have not been established in guidelines; ASE 2017 applies echocardiographic cut-offs to CMR by default. Accumulating outcome data support lower CMR-specific thresholds for hemodynamically significant AR:1,2

    CMR Parameter Threshold Clinical Significance
    RF ≥32–35% Predicts symptom progression, need for AVR, and adverse outcomes
    RF >33% 85% vs 8% progression to surgery (Myerson et al)
    RF ≥43% Optimal CMR threshold — multicenter study n=458 asymptomatic patients (Malahfji et al)1
    RVol >38–47 mL ROC-optimal range for predicting AVR1,2
    Holodiastolic retrograde flow Descending aorta 2.8× risk of death or HF hospitalization1,2

    CMR vs Echo RF: Key Discordance

    CMR-RF ≥32–35% predicts adverse outcomes — substantially lower than the echo-derived severe threshold (RF ≥50%). RF ≥50% has high specificity but poor sensitivity for identifying early LV remodeling by CMR. Among patients within guideline thresholds by echo (LVESDi <25 mm/m²), >1/3 had LVESVi ≥45 mL/m² on CMR, placing them at elevated risk.

    CMR LV Remodeling Parameters

    Parameter Threshold Significance
    LVESVi ≥43 mL/m² 2–3× higher adverse outcome risk (Malahfji)1
    LVESVi ≥45 mL/m² Independent predictor of death/HF with preserved EF; ESC/EACTS 2025 Class IIb trigger2
    LVEDVi ≥109 mL/m² Associated with adverse outcomes (Malahfji)1
    LVEDVi >155 mL/m² Predicts poor reverse remodeling post-AVR2
    CMR-GLS Worse than −16% to −19% Abnormal strain; associated with adverse outcomes1
    Indexed ECV ≥24 mL/m² Associated with mortality and progression to AVR1

    Volumes Outperform Linear Dimensions

    LVESDi >25 mm/m² (current guideline threshold) misses >1/3 of patients with CMR LVESVi ≥45 mL/m². Measure true volumetric indices — diameter is a surrogate. ESC/EACTS 2025 now formally incorporates LVESVi alongside linear triggers.

    Secondary (Functional) MR

    Secondary MR causes adverse outcomes at substantially lower regurgitant burden than primary MR:

    Parameter Hemodynamically Significant (ESC/EACVI) Intervention Threshold (ACC/AHA — COAPT criteria)
    EROA ≥0.20 cm² ≥0.30 cm²
    RVol ≥30 mL ≥30 mL

    Primary vs Secondary MR: Do Not Apply the Same Thresholds

    EROA ≥0.40 cm² and RVol ≥60 mL define severe primary MR. In secondary MR, EROA ≥0.20 cm² (ESC/EACVI) represents hemodynamically significant disease with adverse outcomes. An EROA of 0.30–0.35 cm² is moderate–severe in primary MR but constitutes the ACC/AHA intervention threshold in secondary MR.

    CMR Assessment

    Method: LV stroke volume − Ao net forward flow = MR regurgitant volume. This indirect method is the reference standard. Phase-contrast directly at the mitral valve is less reliable due to through-plane valve motion.

    CMR outcome data in primary MR (Myerson et al):

    CMR Parameter Threshold Outcome
    RVol ≤55 mL 91% 5-year survival without surgery
    RVol >55 mL 21% 5-year survival without surgery
    RF ≤40% Favorable long-term outcome
    RF >40% Adverse outcomes — parallel to RVol >55 mL

    CMR RF Threshold for MR

    CMR RF >40% predicts adverse outcomes in primary MR — lower than the echo-derived severe threshold of RF ≥50%. The same direction of discordance seen in AR applies: CMR detects hemodynamically significant disease at lower RF values than echo-based criteria.

    5-Tier TR Grading: Clinical Context

    Massive and torrential grades were developed because most patients enrolled in transcatheter tricuspid valve intervention (TTVI) trials had TR severity above traditional severe thresholds. These grades carry independent prognostic weight: massive TR (VC >9.2 mm by outcomes analysis) is associated with greater RV adverse remodeling and increased mortality vs lower-grade severe TR. The 2025 ACC Expert Consensus recommends referral to advanced heart disease specialists for moderate or greater TR with progressive heart failure symptoms.

    RVSP estimation (TR jet): RVSP = 4 × V²TR + RAP

    RAP estimate: 3 mmHg (normal IVC), 8 mmHg (borderline), 15 mmHg (dilated, non-collapsing IVC)
